## Invocation teardown observation

The observer is opt-in and compiled under `#[cfg(test)]`. It attaches to the attempt's `InvocationBudget` and follows all nested `Runtime` clones. Each successful reservation gets a unique lease ID and per-lease live token. `InvocationStore::new` records a Store-created ID and a `Weak<ResourceTable>` for that Store's own table. After the Store field drops, its test observer verifies that its own weak reference cannot upgrade while its own lease token is still live, then records table destruction. `InvocationLease::drop` clears that ID's token and records lease release. Keep the existing single-Store unit probe and its `budget.live() == 1` assertion unchanged. The new observer uses per-ID ordering, so concurrent nested Stores do not depend on the global live count being one.

Role attribution uses both a test-only attempt phase copied through `Runtime` clones and the contract address at `invoke_unsettled`, before `make_store`. The `nested_chain` role applies only during the target invocation phase to addresses in the exact published error-test/proxy chain. Native settlement calls during that phase receive a named `target_native_auxiliary` role. Measurement calls before the target, after the target, and after rollback receive separate `pre_observation`, `post_observation`, and `rollback_observation` roles. Direct Store construction in an active attempt must declare a reviewed role. Unknown or unassigned roles fail. This prevents native balance, floor, supply, and `storage-state()` calls from being counted as guest chain frames.

Source trace for the roles: `Runtime::make_store` reserves the lease, clones the runtime, creates a new table, and constructs `InvocationStore` in `core/indexer/src/runtime/mod.rs`. `invoke_unsettled` in `core/indexer/src/runtime/call.rs` checks the depth gate before calling `make_store` and has the contract address needed for classification. `run_chain_probe` in `core/indexer/src/runtime/call/tests/sequential_chains.rs` publishes the chain before the attempt, then calls balance, floor, and supply queries around the target, `storage-state()` after the target, and the same queries and `storage-state()` after rollback.
